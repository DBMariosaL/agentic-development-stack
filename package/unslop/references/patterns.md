# Unslop pattern catalog

Use these patterns diagnostically. Fix them when present and harmful. Do not force changes where the original wording is already natural.

## Content

1. **Puffery.** Cut phrases such as "pivotal moment", "testament to", "evolving landscape", "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", and "deeply rooted". State what happened.
2. **Name-dropping.** Do not list media outlets or authorities without explaining what they contribute. Pick the relevant source and state the point.
3. **Superficial -ing phrases.** Remove empty tails such as "highlighting", "ensuring", "reflecting", "showcasing", and "fostering". Either explain the mechanism or cut the phrase.
4. **Promotional language.** Replace words such as "nestled", "vibrant", "breathtaking", "groundbreaking", "renowned", "stunning", and "must-visit" with concrete description unless promotion is explicitly requested.
5. **Vague attribution.** Replace "experts believe", "industry reports suggest", or "some critics argue" with a named source, or remove the attribution.
6. **Formulaic challenge framing.** Replace constructions such as "Despite challenges, X continues to thrive" with the specific facts.

## Language

7. **Default AI vocabulary.** Prefer plain alternatives to words such as additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape in the abstract, pivotal, showcase, tapestry in the abstract, testament, underscore, and vibrant.
8. **Fancy substitutes for "is" or "has".** Avoid "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", and similar inflation when "is" or "has" is clearer.
9. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the actual point directly.
10. **Forced rule of three.** Do not manufacture groups of three for rhetorical symmetry. Use the natural number of points.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Do not rotate terms merely to avoid repetition. Keep one precise term when it remains the right term.
12. **False ranges.** Avoid "from X to Y" when X and Y are not endpoints on a meaningful scale. Name the topics directly.

## Style

13. **Em dash overuse.** Prefer periods or commas. Do not substitute a different dash merely to preserve the same habit.
14. **Colon overuse.** Use colons for genuine lists, examples, labels, or definitions. Do not use them as decorative mid-sentence connectors.
15. **Boldface overuse.** Use emphasis only when it improves navigation or meaning.
16. **Inline-header list repetition.** Avoid bullets where a bold label restates the sentence that follows. Either use real headings or write direct prose.
17. **Title case headings.** Prefer sentence case unless a style guide requires otherwise.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove them unless the user or medium clearly calls for them.
19. **Curly quote normalization.** Prefer straight quotes when plain-text consistency matters. Preserve the user's required typography if specified.

## Communication artifacts

20. **Chatbot phrases.** Remove stock phrases such as "I hope this helps", "Of course", "Certainly", "Great question", and generic invitations to ask more when they add nothing.
21. **Cutoff disclaimers.** Do not hide missing evidence behind phrases such as "while specific details are limited". Retrieve the missing information when required, or state the gap plainly.
22. **Sycophantic tone.** Do not praise the user by default. Respond to the substance.

## Filler

23. **Filler phrases.** Shorten "in order to" to "to", "due to the fact that" to "because", and delete phrases such as "it is important to note that" when they carry no content.
24. **Excessive hedging.** Keep only the uncertainty that is epistemically necessary.
25. **Generic conclusions.** Remove endings such as "the future looks bright". End on the specific result, action, or implication.

## Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Prefer concrete terms over fashionable abstractions such as substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive used as a noun, harness used metaphorically, surface in phrases like "API surface", bedrock, scaffolding used metaphorically, modality, paradigm, gold-plating, ratchet used metaphorically, evacuate for moving code, endgame, north star, and flywheel. Keep a technical term only when it is genuinely standard and precise in the domain.

## Plain speech

27. **Say what it does, not how it feels.** Replace vague experiential claims with the mechanism, instruction, observable behavior, or number. If a sentence could be copied unchanged into another project's documentation, it is probably too generic.
28. **Dense sentences.** Split sentences that require backtracking. Prefer one main idea per sentence when clarity improves.
29. **Passive voice without reason.** Name the actor when the actor matters. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally de-emphasized.
30. **Adverb-propped weak verbs.** Remove the adverb, strengthen the verb, or state the measured result.
31. **Fancy synonym for a plain word.** Prefer "use" to "utilize" or "leverage", "help" to "facilitate", "many" to "numerous", and "if" to "in the event that" when meaning is unchanged.

## Human voice without fabrication

Removing tells is only half the job. Sterile prose can sound just as synthetic.

- Use specific details already supported by the task or sources.
- Vary rhythm naturally.
- Acknowledge real complexity when the evidence supports it.
- Use a clear point of view when the task calls for judgment.
- Allow a little structural irregularity rather than polishing every paragraph into the same shape.
- Never invent a lived experience, emotional reaction, or personal anecdote merely to create "soul".
